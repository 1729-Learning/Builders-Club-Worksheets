## Choosing the hardware

This fed the feasibility reasons on the §3 Must cards. The constraints were no professional installation, nothing per student to charge, and almost no desk space.

| Option | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|
| Ceiling cameras, drilled in | Nothing on desks | Purchase plus install cost, facilities sign-off, and they film faces |
| A document camera on every desk | The sharpest image | 8–30 devices to wire or charge, cable clutter, ~$70–100 each |
| Student phone or Chromebook camera | Already owned | Takes desk space, fiddly angles, phone bans, devices vary |
| Smart pens (dot-paper digitizers) | Perfect strokes and replay, no camera | Special paper, charging, $100+ a pen, pens get lost |
| **One 4K webcam on a boom stand over the table** | One device, USB to the tutor's laptop, ~$150, set up in 5 minutes | Reads about 6–8 papers at most; hands and heads block the view |
| One clamp or boom camera per desk pod (to scale to 30) | ~$150 a pod, no drilling, wall or USB power | Needs a small hub or PC per room |

The MVP uses the boom camera. The class-of-30 version is pod mode (a Should). Smart pens are a Could.

## Why each answer works

- **1. Problems in my life**: Ten small, boring, real problems from one week. Most come from watching other people, and nobody is solving anything yet.
- **1. Pick your top 3 by impact**: Each pick says how often it happens, what it costs and who feels it. The bus and the printer are annoying but they don’t make the cut.
- **1. Transform 3 problems into problem statements**: Each one names a narrow group, a root cause and a cost you could check by asking. None of them mentions a camera or an app. #1 is strongest because its cost is measured in minutes.
- **1. Write your problem hypothesis**: It covers who, how often, today’s workaround and why that falls short. The guesses are labeled as guesses, so interviews can prove them wrong.
- **1. Build your interview questions**: Every question asks about something that already happened, and none of them hints at a fix. The survey question takes one tap, and “not sure” is a useful answer too.

- **1. Your real problem statement**: It has three lines: the statement, TODAY and WHY THAT FAILS. Every claim can be checked by asking a tutor, and there’s still no product in it.
